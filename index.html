README.TXT

What Racks is.

readme.txt · Notepad_□×

Racks is one coin, one pool and 100 server racks. The pool's liquidity is cut into 100 price bands stacked end to end, and each band belongs to one rack. When the price is inside a band, that rack is running: every swap there pays it the fee, and nothing else ever pays a rack.

A rack is an NFT with three parts. Eight bytes of spec written at mint from the last job hash, where byte 0 is the clock and sets the swap fee between 0.80% and 1.51%. An account of its own where SOL fees land and travel with it when it sells. And its band, whose coin fees are held in memory and compacted back into the band as locked liquidity.

Bit flips. A swap over the flip threshold rolls one bit of the running rack's spec against its ECC byte. A landed flip can raise or lower a fee, move a rack across a class line, or only change how it looks.

Locked for good. Mint payments, sale royalties and compacted coin stay in the pool. Nothing runs on a clock. If nobody trades, nothing moves.
